My research is organised around a common question: how does geometry control the mechanics of slender and hierarchical structures? I develop analytical, computational, and experimental approaches that connect local frame descriptions and hierarchical geometry to contact, stiffness, instability, and structural response.

1. Geometric mechanics of hierarchical slender structures

From local frames to recursive helical geometry

A current focus is the geometric description of multi-level helical and filamentary structures. I am developing Hierarchical Bishop-Frame Mapping (HBFM), a framework that uses rotation-minimising (Bishop) frames to separate carrier geometry from material or orbital phase during recursive construction. The aim is to preserve intended pitch, phase, topology, and material coordinates across hierarchy levels while providing geometry that can be projected consistently to beam, shell, solid, and fabrication models.

Topics: Bishop/RMF frames · hierarchical helices · topology · geometry generation · mesh-ready modelling

2. Contact, instability, and mechanics of helical assemblies

How local interaction produces global structural behaviour

Helical assemblies develop strongly coupled responses through contact, friction, relative sliding, and geometric constraint. My work investigates how contact networks and local geometry influence effective stiffness, stick-slip response, mode transitions, and bifurcation. An important direction is to identify mechanics that remain common across ropes, cables, layered helical structures, and architected slender systems.

Topics: contact mechanics · friction · stick-slip · bifurcation · mode transition · homogenised response

3. Multiscale mechanics of marine cables and flexible structures

Efficient local analysis of structurally complex systems

My doctoral and continuing research develops reduced and repeated-unit-cell models for submarine power cables and related flexible structures. These methods combine periodic boundary conditions, specialised representations of helical components, and finite-element modelling to retain local mechanical detail at substantially lower computational cost than full-scale models. The framework has been extended from tension and bending to torsion and coupled thermo-electro-mechanical analysis.

Topics: repeated unit cells · homogenisation · finite elements · submarine power cables · multiphysics

4. Physical and computational validation

Connecting models to measurable geometry and mechanics

I use finite-element simulation together with physical and image-based validation to test modelling assumptions. Current interests include additive manufacturing for controlled structural specimens, CT/tomography for internal geometry and contact assessment, and numerical continuation for tracing nonlinear equilibria and bifurcations.

Topics: finite elements · numerical continuation · additive manufacturing · CT/tomography · model validation

Earlier and application-driven research

Fibreglass-reinforced flexible pipes

I developed analytical and numerical models in which the fibreglass reinforcement and polyethylene matrix are represented separately rather than homogenised as a single laminate. This enables matrix plasticity and fibreglass failure to be considered explicitly under tension and internal pressure, with experimental validation.

Other structural-mechanics applications

Earlier projects also include metallic strip flexible pipes, reeling analysis, wire-rope mechanics, and pressure-vessel failure. These studies provided the application background from which my broader interest in the mechanics of slender, layered, and hierarchical structures developed.
